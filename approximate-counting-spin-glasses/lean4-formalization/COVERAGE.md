## Scope and deliberate choices

- The theorem covers fixed p ≥ 2, zero external field, finite real couplings, N ≥ p and β ∈ [0,B] strictly below the stated variational threshold. The SK threshold is proved to equal one.
- Symmetry is invariance of the law under negation. Unit second moment includes integrability; no integrability conclusion is obtained by exploiting Lean's default value for a nonintegrable integral. No Gaussianity, density or absence of zero atoms is assumed by the main theorem.
- The final failure estimate retains τ_N under finite variance. Finite fourth moment and higher moments are introduced only in their respective corollaries.
- The formal corollaries use δ_N = (1/2)N^(−b), an admissible schedule proving the paper's stated conclusions; the displayed intermediate confidence schedules in the paper need not be copied literally.
- The arithmetic theorem uses exact real elementary-function oracles, finite comparisons, index/register operations and uniform color draws. The implementation refinements certify the mathematical schedule, not the Lean evaluator's machine instruction count. Numerical stability, finite-precision rounding, bit complexity, practical running time and optimized constants are outside the paper's claim and this certificate.
- The mathematical ambient array/tape contains unused coordinates for convenient finite-product proofs. Input restriction and color marginal results show that these coordinates need not be read or sampled. Concrete bounded generators avoid enumerating the full ambient powerset.
- General mixtures, external fields, critical-temperature endpoints and higher-order ordered tuples with repeated indices are not covered. In particular, repeated indices for p ≥ 3 cannot silently be discarded.
- The appendix's statements about historical Python regression executions and the bibliography are not kernel-certified mathematical propositions. The formal general identities cover the corresponding algebraic behavior; the certificate does not claim to rerun or authenticate those historical scripts.
